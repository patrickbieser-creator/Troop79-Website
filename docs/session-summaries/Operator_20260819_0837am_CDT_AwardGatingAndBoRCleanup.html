What Was Accomplished

Admin nav reorg & roster editor treatment (v1.50.0)

Planned and prototyped by Brad (UX/UI). Records — grown to 7 items, nearly twice any other section — split into Records (Universal Ledger, Roster, Submit & Present, MB Progress) and a new Reports & Exports section (Audits, Weekly Report, Court of Honor, Scoutbook Export), absorbing the old orphaned single-item Output section. Roster moved up to sit directly under Universal Ledger, Patrick's own call over Brad's original placement. A real mobile bug was fixed along the way: section labels had been fully display: none below 800px, collapsing the whole sidebar into one undifferentiated row of chips for anyone on a phone or tablet — exactly where several leaders actually use this nav at troop meetings.

scout-form.tsx's six sections (Identity, Demographics, Contact, Things We Should Know, Parents/Guardians, Status) moved from a plain dashed-divider treatment to numbered section-card chrome plus a sticky scroll-spy rail — additive-only CSS, so none of the ~7 other Lookups editors sharing the same stylesheet were touched.

Dead-auth-gate sweep

Real production bug, found and closed: Scoutbook Export and Roster Import were both still gated on the fully-retired shared-password session check — a leftover from before the app's capability system existed. Both pages were completely unreachable via any grant on Access & Permissions, even though every mutation behind them was already correctly capability-gated. Converted both to requireCapability(), matching every already-converted sibling page. Every other reference to the legacy check in the codebase was swept and confirmed either a legitimate dual-path fallback or dead comment text — no further instances remain.

Access & Permissions and Media Manager fixes

The Access & Permissions table sorted by "most capabilities held first" — a single grant or revoke could reshuffle the whole table mid-edit. Now stable alphabetical order, always. The Media Manager's thumbnail hover overlay had its metadata text rendering in the transparent half of its own darkening gradient, unreadable over any light-colored photo — anchored to the dark edge instead. qa-lead reviewed the access-control changes: SHIP, no blockers.

Merit badge award gating made conditional

Checking "Full merit badge earned" used to be rejected unless every catalog requirement was already completed or also checked in the same batch — forcing a leader to hand-check every individual requirement (cluttering the ledger with extra rows) just to record a badge earned as one fact, e.g. a summer-camp blue card. Fixed through two rounds of Patrick's own clarification: a clean slate (zero requirement activity for that badge) now bypasses the gate entirely; partial progress still requires full satisfaction, unchanged. Rank award gating was deliberately left untouched — a mirrored fix was proposed and explicitly rejected, since ranks are signed off progressively and a Board of Review reviews already-completed work, unlike a merit badge counselor's sign-off.

Board of Review side door closed

The deeper find: every rank with a real Board of Review had TWO independent ways to record it — a properly-gated synthesized award row in the picker, and a real leftover rank_requirements catalog row that auto-created the award via a database trigger with zero leaf-satisfaction check of its own. Checking that leftover row was a complete side door around Patrick's stated rule: "There's never a scenario where a requirement is missing and a BoR is valid."

Removed the redundant catalog row (all 6 ranks) and its matching descriptive-text row via a production migration, verified directly against production data before and after — not just local. 4 historical scouts using the old code path all already had their own correctly-dated award, completely unaffected; 1 approved family Board of Review proof submission was left in place. A rollback dump of the exact pre-migration production values was taken first, as a safety net, per an Opus pre-deploy review. Two more things were fixed along the way: Scout rank's terminal requirement (Scoutmaster Conference — Scout has no real BoR) had no equivalent gate at all and now does; and the public Clipboard, which had its own separate dependency on the same catalog row, would have silently lost its "Board of Review" line for all 6 ranks — now synthesizes it from the award record directly instead.
