Started as a one-line bug report from the live Proof Queue. Ended with a feature that closes the loop the bug exposed: families never heard back about anything they submitted.

What was accomplished

Duplicate “I did this” submissions v1.126.0

Scouts’ proof submissions were appearing many times over. The production data settled what kind of bug it was — genuinely duplicated rows, not a display fan-out — and showed two different human behaviours behind it:

ScoutRequirementRowsSpacingWhat it was
AnjaliFirst Class 9a30.6–1.0sA double-tap
HenryFirst Class 7d153–5s over 65sClicking, waiting, clicking again

Three gaps let it through: no disable on the submit button (the action uploads media and emails a leader before redirecting, so it looked dead), a bare insert with no idempotency check, and an index on the right three columns that was not unique. Every duplicate was a rank requirement, because merit badge pages already hid the claim icon once a proof was pending while rank pages never checked at all.

Fixed in four layers: a pending-aware submit button, a partial unique index (WHERE status = 'pending', so returned and approved claims stay re-submittable), replace-in-place on a genuine redo, and “Already sent” on the rank page.

Full-size proof photos v1.126.1

Asked mid-review how to see a submitted photo full size. The answer was that you couldn’t — the thumbnail was a bare 160px image with no link, no lightbox, nothing to click. Now opens the original in a new tab. The signed-URL lifetime went from 10 minutes to an hour at the same time: at 10 minutes, clicking a photo after reading a few submissions would have failed and the fix would have looked broken.

Library admin: shelves and publishing v1.127.0

The Topics/shelves tab was the one admin edit form the August save standard never reached — a permanently-live Save that looked identical whether or not anything had been typed, and no way back from a half-made edit. It now behaves like every other form, with Retire/Restore deliberately left outside the dirty gate as a one-click action.

Publishing a resource with no shelf placed is now refused rather than silently producing something live that appears on no page — and it names the likelier mistake (choosing a shelf but never pressing “+ Place”) separately from the generic one.

Review notifications v1.128.0

New feature. A leader approving, returning or rejecting something now emails the person who submitted it: what you sent, what happened, and a link. Until now a submission went into a queue and nothing ever came back.

  • Proof claims name the requirement and whether you sent a photo, a link or a write-up — never the write-up body or the photo itself.
  • Profile updates name which details changed and never what you put in them, so medical notes stay out of email exactly as they already do on the way in.
  • Leader-filed claims notify the leader — they are who submitted, and a leader who filed a dozen at a meeting is who most needs the reminder.

Sending is allowed here because a leader clicking a review control is the explicit trigger the email rules require — one recipient, an address already on the roster, nothing batched or scheduled.

Caught in review

Four defects were found by QA review or by a test rather than in production. Two of them were mine, in work I had already called finished.

Silent data loss, blocked before shipping. The first duplicate fix short-circuited on any pending submission with no time limit and showed the success screen. A scout who rewrote a weak answer days later, while a leader hadn’t yet looked at the first one, would have had that work thrown away and still been told “Sent” — a worse bug than the duplicates it was fixing. Now the newer submission replaces the older one and nothing is discarded.
The deploy order was backwards. I had planned to ship the notification code before its migration. Because that code writes the new column, code-first would have failed every “I did this” submission against a column that didn’t exist yet — breaking the whole flow, not just notifications. The rule that holds: code-first for a tightening, database-first for an additive column the code writes.
Also caughtWhy it mattered
Rejection notices labelled raw database keys Approvals filtered through the allowlist; rejections didn’t, and the labeller passes an unrecognised key through verbatim — one bypass from an arbitrary string reaching an inbox.
Returning a proof had no race guard Harmless while it only overwrote a status. Once it sends mail, losing that race emails a family “needs another look” about a claim another leader just approved.
Dead email addresses came back to life The new single-address resolver fell back to a cached column that knows nothing about deliverability, handing back an address that had already bounced. Caught by its own test.
A form control with no accessible name The shelf picker reached screen readers unnamed. Surfaced because a test couldn’t find the control by name — the test failure was the real bug.
