What Was Accomplished

The ask: a Utilities tool that re-sums every transaction for an independent audit. Then the real problem: “Recurring incidents where the ledger does not balance with the bank accounts online. I am more suspicious of my ledger than I am of the bank.” Before any code, a read-only pass over the 9/22 backup showed the arithmetic was sound — and that every “balanced” state so far had been forced with plug adjustments.

The $100, found

  • Patrick exported two years of Landmark checking and savings as CSV. Comparing ledger and bank balances day by day, most gaps were timing and closed within days — one never did.
  • Ledger #1878 (Sept 12, 2025, −$100 “Samoset Council - Reservation”, CashFlow row 258) has no bank counterpart. The bank shows only the $200 Samoset deposit (#1877). The ledger ran $100 under the bank from that week until the +$100 plug of Aug 20, 2026.

v1.132.0 — Utilities → Ledger Audit (also in the Finance menu)

  • Bank Match — upload a Landmark export; every bank row is matched to a ledger row by amount and date (checks get 60 days to clear), batched Venmo cash-outs and mobile deposits are matched as exact-sum groups, and near misses (swapped digits, flipped sign, off by cents) are called out. It lists what’s in the ledger but not at the bank and vice versa, and shows where each lasting gap opened with the rows dated there. The file is read once and never stored.
  • Ledger Checks — a new Postgres function re-adds every account and scout independently of the app (matches to the cent, 662 rows); reconciliations re-checked as of their own dates; rows changed after a period was reconciled; plugs; one-sided checking↔savings transfers; likely duplicates; reimbursement payouts; rows booked backwards.
  • Fixed: the Financial Ledger’s reconciliation “drift” compared today’s balance to the last statement — checking read “drift $-644.58” when the truth was −$100. It now measures as of the statement date.
