What Was Accomplished

The ask: scouts work individually and save menu plans behind a scout sign-in; leaders can see them; scouts upload recipes others can use, with credit. It serves Second Class, First Class and the Cooking merit badge, for home and troop cooking.

The plan: Plans/Menu-Monster-Scout-Workspace.md

  • Patrick’s rules:
    • Menus are kept with no time limit; cleanup is a backlog item.
    • Any admin adult and the scout’s parents can read menus, and menu actions are logged in the audit log.
    • One scout’s name per menu, plus an optional patrol name.
    • Recipes are credited “Sam K.”
    • Scouts fix prices after shopping and adjust items on the fly.
  • Phases: saved menus → on-the-fly edits and prices paid → requirement checklist, sharing, and the leader and parent views → scout recipe submissions.
  • Proposed and awaiting Patrick:
    • Shared menus appear on the shelf and on their outing’s page; outsiders see the patrol name only.
    • A ±50% sanity check on scout-entered prices.
    • New shared recipes still go through a leader.

The design, iterated live

  • First two passes: Brad’s first prototype was too busy, and a softer second pass still carried too much.
  • Patrick’s Claude Design mock: he brought a mock and handoff of the scout recipe editor, and Operator built it as a working page.
  • Live tuning, round by round:
    • The ingredient list shows totals to buy for the meal’s headcount, so it reads as a rough shopping list. Chili for 8 shows 2 lb beef and 2 cans of beans, and the totals follow the headcount.
    • A Total / Per person switch sits on the heading line, and amounts stay in a right-hand column.
    • Ingredients and steps can be dragged to reorder, and step numbers follow the drag.
    • Clicking an ingredient opens its shopping details.
    • Lists are tighter, extra labels are gone, and Save and Submit sit on the title line.
  • Rollout to every page:
    • Jenna turned the approved page into a brief for the others: rules, cuts and merges.
    • Brad rebuilt every page on shared styles and components.
    • Three navigation options sit behind a switch for Patrick to compare.
Prototype check (Brad): every page loaded under every viewer and navigation option with no console errors and no sideways scrolling, and the main flows were run by hand. Not checked: a real phone, a screen reader, or a keyboard-only walk.
