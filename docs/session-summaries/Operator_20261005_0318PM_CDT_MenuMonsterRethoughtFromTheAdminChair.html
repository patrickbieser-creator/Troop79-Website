What Was Accomplished

The outcome: a leader can add foods and recipes without knowing the data model or having shopped yet. A single food is one entry, a price is one small form, nothing needs a price to go on the menu, and a leader can sit beside a scout and fix the scout’s menu directly.

Releases

  • v1.164.0: a scout account can pay an event fee for anyone in the scout’s household; “Whose account” in both Record payment dialogs.
  • v1.165.0: the yield typed for a package is remembered as that food’s conversion; nine conversions pre-filled.
  • v1.166.0: a read-only Conversions tab on every saved menu, written as a lesson for scouts.
  • v1.167.0–v1.168.0: the Price book shows each brand with its prices as one list, has one add form, opens an ingredient under the row clicked, and can rename an ingredient.
  • v1.169.0: “Make it a single food” on a recipe; the Cookies entry that could not be opened was repaired.
  • v1.170.0–v1.171.0: leaders have full rights on a scout’s menu (edit, duplicate, delete, share); the scout is told a leader changed it.
  • v1.172.0: a single food is one entry: the food and its menu item are tied, share one name, and retire together. “On the menu by itself” lives on the food in the Price book.
  • v1.173.0: foods and recipes publish without a price; menus mark what is not priced.
  • v1.174.0: What we bought lists foods with no price, so the receipt can give the first one.
  • v1.175.0: a food’s first price goes live at once; adding a price for a counted food asks one question.
Caught on the way: one commit went out under a release message while carrying only a file move, because a failed git add had its error hidden (explained by the next commit; history not rewritten). A claim that new recipes could overwrite Cookies was wrong and was corrected in the same hour. Most screens were verified by automated tests; only the Conversions tab and the Price book were looked at in a browser.
