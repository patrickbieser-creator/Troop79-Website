What Was Accomplished

The outcome: a store receipt is now data in the troop’s own database, every line arrives with a proposed match to the shopping list, and the scouts have a step of their own where they confirm each line, place the add-ons on a meal, and read every meal twice — as planned and as bought — with the cost per meal and per person and the difference in words. The drudgery is gone; the judgement per line stays with the scouts.

Gear once per meal, steps open, the letters column (v1.204.0)

An open food no longer lists gear of its own; the meal’s one Gear list carries it. The “Steps · Gear” toggle is gone and a food’s steps are simply there. The … menu stays on the food’s line even under a diet warning. Every gear row, on the meal panel and the Gear step, carries five fixed-width slots — B L D S and Ds — so the letters line up down the list. Patrick: “excellent.”

The Chef kit (v1.205.0)

A fourth place gear lives, between Patrol box and Bring from home, with the list declared once so a fifth is one line plus a migration. The database check was widened first.

The cook sheet (v1.206.0, v1.206.1)

“Print plan” on the Meals and Review steps opens a page of its own with no site chrome: every day and meal in two columns, each food’s ingredients, its steps printed once with later meals pointing back, the meal’s gear on one line, diet notes. Patrick’s first printout trimmed it to the big picture — names only, no quantities or brands or prices, a single food just its name, bigger headings, one indent, diets in italics, a meal kept to one column.

Receipts become data (v1.207.0)

Two photos of the Aldi receipt (93 items, $280.26 against a $368.43 plan) were transcribed, identical lines coalesced, the sum checked against the printed subtotal and the candy tax. Two tables hold receipts and their lines as printed; a matcher expands Aldi’s abbreviations and proposes the planned food for every line it can defend, leaving the ten add-ons unidentified; a reconcile library gives each meal as planned and as bought, splitting shared lines by each meal’s share. An import script loads a transcribed receipt; the rows reached production through the database pooler because this machine holds no production service key.

The Receipt step — Planned vs bought (v1.208.0)

In the menu’s tab set, readable by everyone who can open the menu. One list in receipt order with only the first pending line open: Confirm, Something else…, Not on the plan (pick the meal it joined), Set aside, and a persistent Undo; focus moves to the next line and a status line announces. A confirmed line writes onto the ingredient’s What-we-bought line, tagged with its receipt line so Undo removes exactly that item. Below, one table per meal — Food, As planned, As bought — with the meal’s cost and per-person cost in both shapes and the difference said in words, add-ons and not-bought lines called out, a totals row against the receipt, and four prompts for the scouts to compare and contrast. Jenna shaped the interaction; qa-lead caught the Undo collision and a page that dropped the step.
