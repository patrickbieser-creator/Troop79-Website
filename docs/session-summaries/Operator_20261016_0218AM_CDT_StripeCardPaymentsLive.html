What Was Accomplished

The outcome: customers can pay for wreaths by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay on Stripe's secure page. Venmo and PayPal stay first and are the default. Every payment books itself in the finance ledger: the full amount as fundraiser income, plus Stripe's fee as a separate expense. A full end-to-end run (pay, fee, refund) passed against the sandbox, and Patrick's first live card payment went through.

Wreath sale: card payments (Release B)

Stripe's implementation planner chose hosted Checkout with a webhook. tech-lead and qa-lead reviewed the plan and then the code, and every required change was made:

  • Webhook effects and their record of having seen the event are written in one database step.
  • The amount paid is re-checked against the order.
  • A card payment that lands on a cancelled or already-paid order is queued so exactly that payment can be refunded.
  • Any open payment page is expired when an order changes.
  • Starting a card payment is rate-limited.

The optional card-fee cover is now calculated so the troop keeps the whole amount. The sale's fee setting moved to Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30.

Going live needed three key fixes in Vercel:

  • A trailing line break in the key. The site now trims both keys when it reads them.
  • The publishable key pasted in place of the secret key.
  • Mixed sandbox and live settings.

Production now runs a live restricted key and a live webhook.

Wreath sale: books and orders

Mark paid books a checking / fundraiser "Wreaths '26" row in the finance ledger. The 3 cancelled test orders were deleted, and Sara Zirbel's order became W-1001.

News

Story excerpts accept markdown. A link works on the home page's lead story; on cards, which already open the story, only its words show. Search previews get plain text.

Behind the scenes

The local database's migration history was repaired after checking it against production. The nightly backup and local copy were run by hand after Docker had been down.
