What Was Accomplished

The outcome: Patrick no longer has to guess who is coming and who can teach. The design is a single board per meeting where a scout's pick and an adult's offer meet. Matching runs live up to Sunday 5:30 pm, and the result drops into the meeting editor for him to approve.

The board

  • Scouts, often with a parent, pick up to five things in earning order, with optional ranking. Picks carry forward until taught.
  • Everyone says Coming, Not sure yet, or Can't make it. A scout who can't come can still pick for a later meeting.
  • Adults, parents and Junior Leaders commit to one or two topics, or post an offer across several dates.
  • Each scout gets at most two topics. Merit badge nights use adult-assigned station rotations, and campout prep and parties have no board.
  • A public TV board shows sessions and names live during the meeting.

Who can teach

  • Teaching skills move to people records, so parents count. A "What I can teach" page lets each person keep their own skills current. Admin can undo a change but never has to approve one.
  • Any First Class or higher scout, or any high schooler, can offer to teach.

Reviews and prototypes

  • tech-lead said go, with changes to the data model. troop79-specialist found six domain fixes, including that the real requirement unit is the lettered row (Tenderfoot 4a), which was checked in the database. jenna shaped the screens.
  • Brad built the whole flow as static prototypes. Patrick's feedback is in: picks for a later meeting, outing rows fully disabled, and no period after the last initial.
