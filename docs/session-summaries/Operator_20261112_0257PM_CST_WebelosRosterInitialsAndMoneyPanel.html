What Was Accomplished

The outcome: the seven Webelos are on the roster and signed up for Sunday's Climbing night at the scout price. Any person can be given sign-off initials without a database edit. Leaders run an event's money from one ledger with a panel per person, and every payment, amount change and void now records who made it and why.

People

  • Name suffixes: Jr., Sr. and III show after the last name everywhere (“Gabe Helkowski Sr.”). Webelos parent names were corrected from Patrick's list.
  • Sign-off initials: leaders add, rename, retire and restore them on a person's record. Renaming updates every advancement entry signed with the old initials. Retired initials can go to someone new after five years. The suggestion tries three letters (KPi, then KaP) before a number. Kara added hers: KPD.
  • Merges: merging a duplicate now moves their permissions to the person kept. Kara's finance access had been left on her old duplicate record; it was moved, and no other record had the same problem. A merge where both people had initials no longer fails.

Webelos

  • A Webelos tab on the Roster, troop IDs in the next cohort (F01…), sign-up as “Webelos” at the scout price, and advancement tracked but kept out of reports and Clipboards until they cross over.
  • Cross over… makes them an active scout with the same ID and saves their BSA ID. Advancement they earned as a Webelos keeps its dates. Undo crossover reverses a mistake.
  • The seven visiting Webelos were converted. Logan's, Kellan's and Hank's guest sign-ups and payments moved onto their new records.

Event money

  • Guests now carry a price and count toward the household total. Before this, Webelos guests showed as owing $0.
  • The Money tab is now a summary band, a read-only ledger with totals, and a panel for each person. In the panel a leader sets what the person owes (the tier, a custom amount with a reason, or Not paying), records part payments on any past date, refunds, credits an overpayment, and sees the person's history with initials and void reasons. Patrick: “the screen looks good.”
